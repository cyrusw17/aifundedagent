# Liquidity — The Fuel of Price Delivery

## What liquidity means in ICT/SMC
Liquidity = resting orders that can fill large size:
- Stop losses beyond swing highs/lows
- Buy/sell stops of breakout traders
- Pending orders at obvious levels

Smart money narrative: **engineer or seek liquidity, then deliver price the other way.**

## Buy-Side Liquidity (BSL)
- Pooled above highs (stops of shorts, breakout buys)
- Equal highs are especially clean BSL magnets

## Sell-Side Liquidity (SSL)
- Pooled below lows (stops of longs, breakdown sells)
- Equal lows are especially clean SSL magnets

## Common liquidity pools to mark daily
- Previous Day High / Low (PDH / PDL)
- Previous Week High / Low (PWH / PWL)
- Session highs/lows (Asia, London, NY)
- Hourly / 4H swing highs & lows
- Equal highs / equal lows
- Obvious trendline or “retail” levels where stops cluster

## Liquidity sweep / raid
Price **trades beyond** the level (takes the stops) then **rejects** and reverses.
- Sweep ≠ breakout continuation
- High-probability story: sweep → displacement → MSS → entry on retrace

## Inducement (IDM)
A smaller, “obvious” level left inside a range to trap early entries before the true move to external liquidity. Treat shallow pullback highs/lows as possible inducement, not automatic entries.

## Turtle Soup (ICT naming of the classic fade)
Failed breakout: price runs stops beyond a key high/low then reverses. Functionally the same family as Wyckoff spring/upthrust and Raschke Turtle Soup.

## Draw on liquidity
Before the session: ask **where is price likely drawn?**
- That draw becomes your **target narrative**
- Entries are taken *with* that draw, after opposing liquidity is taken
