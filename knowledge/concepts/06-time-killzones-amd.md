# Time — Killzones, Macros, Power of 3 (AMD)

## Why time matters
Institutional participation clusters. A perfect pattern outside active windows is often a trap or dead chop.

## ICT Killzones (common EST/EDT framing)
Exact labels vary by source; the operational idea is constant — trade during high-participation windows:

| Window (approx. ET) | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Asia | Often accumulation / range building |
| London Open Killzone (~2:00–5:00 AM) | Manipulation & early directional moves |
| NY Open Killzone (~7:00–10:00 / 8:30–11:00 AM) | High volatility, news, continuation or reversal of London |
| NY Lunch | Often consolidation / lower quality |
| NY PM | Possible continuation / reversals into close |

Always verify times for your broker timezone and DST.

## Power of 3 — AMD
Daily narrative template:
1. **Accumulation** — tight range (often Asia)
2. **Manipulation** — Judas swing / raid of range liquidity opposite the true move
3. **Distribution** — real directional delivery toward the draw on liquidity

Bullish day sketch: wick/raid below open or Asia low → reverse → close toward highs / BSL  
Bearish day sketch: wick/raid above open or Asia high → reverse → close toward lows / SSL

## Silver Bullet (time-boxed FVG model)
One-hour windows (commonly taught):
- London SB: **3:00–4:00 AM ET**
- NY AM SB: **10:00–11:00 AM ET** (often highest quality)
- NY PM SB: **2:00–3:00 PM ET**

Inside the window: liquidity event → displacement → FVG → entry on FVG retrace, aligned with bias.

## Macros
Short timed algorithms ICT references (e.g., specific :50–:10 style windows). Useful advanced filter; not required for a clean core model.
