# TJR Teachings — Notes & Distillation

TJR (Tyler J. Riches) teaches a **discretionary SMC framework** heavily influenced by ICT, simplified for practical day trading. Emphasis: order flow change after liquidity is taken, multi-timeframe confluence, psychology, journaling, and risk.

> Community note: Critics call it “repackaged ICT.” Practically, TJR’s value is **compression into a repeatable routine**, not inventing new market physics.

## Core process (from TJR “how to trade” framing)

1. **Mark key levels**
   - Previous session highs/lows
   - Hourly highs/lows
   - 4H highs/lows
   - Other obvious liquidity (PDH/PDL, equals)
2. **Wait for a sweep** of one of those levels after the market/session is active
3. **Drop timeframe** and look for a **change in order flow**, such as:
   - Break of structure / market structure shift
   - Inverse FVG
   - Close through a relevant Fibonacci extension level (TJR references continuation logic around extension context in short-form teaching)
4. **Find continuation confluence**
   - Fair Value Gap
   - Equilibrium
   - Breaker block
   - Order block
5. **Wait for price to enter the confluence zone and push out** in trade direction (candle confirmation)
6. **Stops** beyond the swept high/low
7. **Take profits** at previous key levels / opposing liquidity

## Path-to-profitability thought process (expanded)

### 1) Daily / HTF bias
- Read 4H (and higher) structure
- Note whether imbalances are respected or disrespected
- Use Asia / London / prior day extremes as anchors

### 2) Identify draws on liquidity
- Levels where orders rest are both **entry fuel** and **targets**

### 3) Confirm order fills
- Sweep of significant high/low
- LTF change in order flow (BOS, inverse FVG, etc. on 5m/1m)

### 4) Continuation
- EQ fills, FVG holds, breaker/OB reactions
- Use LTF to refine earlier entries inside the continuation zone

### 5) Exit
- Scale / target where opposing liquidity should get paid
- Prior session H/L and HTF liquidity draws

## Soft skills TJR stresses (funded-critical)
- Strategy + risk + psychology are a triad; missing one fails the account
- Discretionary skill comes from **chart time** (pattern recognition), not more indicators
- Journal every trade; review mistakes ruthlessly
- No rigid robot sequence of timeframes — adapt, but keep the liquidity → shift → confluence logic
- Bootcamp-style fundamentals before size: psychology, journaling, structure, emotional control

## TJR vs raw ICT (operational difference)

| Dimension | ICT-heavy study | TJR-style ops |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concept surface area | Very large | Compressed checklist |
| Entry language | PD arrays, OTE, macros, many models | Sweep → order-flow change → continuation confluence |
| Timeframes | Strict mentorship models vary | HTF bias + LTF confirmation, flexible |
| Edge claim | Algorithmic delivery literacy | Discretion + repetition + risk |

## What to steal for a funded playbook
1. Key-level map before open  
2. No trade without sweep + order-flow change  
3. Require a second confluence (FVG/EQ/OB/breaker)  
4. Hard stop beyond sweep  
5. TP at next marked liquidity  
6. Journal + screen-time over course-hopping
